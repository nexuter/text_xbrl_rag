# Response-To-Reviewers Outline: Demonstration Scope And Generalizability

## Reviewer Concern

The reviewers may question whether the demonstration evidence is too bounded to support the paper's contribution, or whether design-specific counts are being overread as general evidence about model performance, retrieval-method superiority, or failure-mode prevalence.

## Planned Response

We will emphasize that the revised manuscript treats the evidence package as a layered protocol-validation demonstration rather than a representative empirical test.

## Revision Summary For Response Letter

We revised Section VII and Appendix H to clarify the evidentiary role of each component of the demonstration:

1. The three deep cases support source tracing, selected claim-level examples, and correctness-layer illustration.
2. The six extension cases apply the same two-construct, four-condition design across varied reporting environments to assess bounded protocol applicability.
3. The retrieval-stage sensitivity diagnostics document context-volume and source-composition differences before model inference.
4. The 120-claim independent coding sample evaluates whether source-use and integration variables can be applied consistently.

We also revised the hybrid-condition count discussion to clarify that the 8 of 89 integrated hybrid claims, 61 text-only claims, and 20 XBRL-only claims are design-specific diagnostics. They are not population frequencies and are not used to claim model-performance effects.

## Suggested Response-Letter Language

Thank you for raising the concern that the demonstration evidence could be overread relative to the design. We have revised the manuscript to make the scope of the nine-filer package more precise. The revised paper now characterizes the evidence as a four-layer protocol-validation package rather than as a model-performance study. The deep cases support source tracing and examples; the six extension cases assess bounded protocol applicability across varied reporting settings; the sensitivity diagnostics evaluate retrieval-stage source-composition and context-volume differences; and the independent coding sample evaluates source-use and integration coding reliability.

We also revised the discussion of hybrid-condition source-use counts. The manuscript now states that these counts are design-specific diagnostics within the current model, prompt, and context architecture, not estimated population frequencies. The paper's claim is therefore narrower and methodological: response-level retrieval-condition labels are insufficient because claim-level source use can diverge from the nominal retrieval condition. We do not claim that hybrid retrieval is superior, that XBRL improves model performance, or that the observed source-use proportions generalize to SEC filers or LLM audit systems.

## Remaining Boundary To Preserve

Do not describe the nine-filer package as a robustness test, statistical validation, or prevalence estimate. It is a reviewer-inspectable demonstration of protocol feasibility, traceability, source-use heterogeneity, bounded extension, sensitivity diagnostics, and coding reliability.

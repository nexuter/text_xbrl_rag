# Final Reference Package For AJPT Methodological Submission

## Purpose

This file finalizes the working reference package for the AJPT Methodological Papers submission. The goal is not to maximize the number of citations. The goal is to support the manuscript's final position:

> A methodological paper on research design and data/variable construction for LLM-based auditing research.

## Reference Strategy

The final manuscript should use references selectively. The literature review should establish four claims:

1. AJPT has a tradition of publishing methodological guidance when audit researchers need better criteria for emerging methods.
2. XBRL and textual-analysis research show how structured and unstructured reporting data can be used for accounting research, but they do not study retrieval-created LLM information environments.
3. LLM accounting research generally studies model capability, text-analysis use, financial-reporting use, or output reproducibility; this paper studies what researchers can infer from LLM outputs after retrieval constructs the model's information environment.
4. Audit analytics and AI-in-auditing research shows that technology-enabled information environments matter, but the present paper is about research design rather than audit-practice tools.

## How To Use Emerging Audit-System Papers

Recommended manuscript wording:

> Emerging LLM/RAG audit studies increasingly evaluate systems, agents, compliance verification, or task performance (Berger et al. 2025; Wang et al. 2025; Xing and Meng 2026; Xiong, Han, and Zhang 2024). Our focus is different: we provide criteria for auditing researchers to evaluate whether the retrieval-created information environment supports the inference they draw from LLM outputs.

Do not use these papers to imply that this manuscript contributes a superior audit automation system. The contribution is methodological: research design, data/variable construction, evidence logging, and claim-level validity coding.

# Phase 2 Annotated Bibliography

## Purpose

This annotated bibliography supports the literature review for:

**Retrieval as Research Design in LLM-Based Audit Research: Retrieval-Environment Validity and XBRL-Augmented Retrieval**

The goal is not to summarize all related AI, XBRL, or textual analysis research. The goal is to position the paper against the literatures a reviewer is most likely to use when judging novelty.

## Core Positioning Claim

Prior XBRL studies use structured reporting data to construct firm- or disclosure-level variables. Prior textual analysis studies convert accounting narratives into measures. Prior audit analytics studies examine how auditors process large and complex data. Prior LLM accounting studies evaluate model capabilities or AI use in reporting. This paper differs by moving the unit of analysis to the LLM study design itself and showing that retrieval systems dynamically construct the information environment from which audit-related inferences are drawn.

Short version:

> We move from text as data and XBRL as data to retrieval as research design.

## Literature Review Implications

The literature review should not merely list papers. It should establish three claims:

1. AJPT has a tradition of publishing methodological guidance papers when a research method requires design and evaluation criteria.
2. XBRL and textual analysis literatures provide rich data and measurement tools, but they do not address retrieval-created information environments in LLM audit studies.
3. AI, LLM, and audit analytics literatures show the importance of technology-enabled audit research, but they generally focus on capability, adoption, or data processing rather than retrieval-environment validity.

## Reviewer Risk

If the literature review is too broad, the contribution will look diffuse. The review should be selective and organized around the central contrast:

> prior work studies data, tools, auditors, or models; this paper studies the retrieval-created information environment in LLM audit study design.

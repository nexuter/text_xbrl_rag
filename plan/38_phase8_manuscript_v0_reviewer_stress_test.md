# Phase 8 Manuscript Draft v0 Reviewer Stress Test

## Purpose

This memo reviews `plan/37_phase8_manuscript_draft_v0.md` from the perspective of an AJPT reviewer and records revisions made to strengthen the draft.

## Overall Verdict

Promising but not yet submission-ready.

The v0 draft successfully presents the paper as an audit research methodology paper rather than a technical system paper. The central contribution, retrieval-environment validity, is visible and appropriately bounded. The demonstration is also framed as an inference-shift illustration rather than a performance benchmark.

However, a reviewer would still raise several concerns before viewing the paper as ready for submission.

## Reviewer Concerns and Required Revisions

### Concern 1: The first page states the contribution but could more sharply state what researchers do differently.

Risk:

The paper may read as conceptually interesting but not yet sufficiently actionable for AJPT methodology.

Revision made:

Added a research question and an explicit "After reading this paper, audit researchers should..." paragraph in the introduction.

### Concern 2: Some table placeholders make the draft look underdeveloped.

Risk:

Statements such as "Table 1 should compare..." read like planning notes rather than manuscript text.

Revision made:

Converted the fixed-information-set comparison and validity dimensions into manuscript-ready tables inside the v0 draft. Added compact retrieval typology, inferential permissions, correctness layers, and reporting-validity mapping tables.

### Concern 3: The literature review remains citation-placeholder heavy.

Risk:

The contribution may not yet be sufficiently positioned against top accounting and auditing literature.

Revision made:

Kept citation placeholders but sharpened the positioning language. Final citation replacement remains an open task before submission.

### Concern 4: The demonstration could still be perceived as cherry-picked.

Risk:

Selected examples are persuasive but may look opportunistic unless the selection logic and full evidence trail are clear.

Revision made:

Added language that selected examples illustrate pre-specified divergence patterns and that the full 94-claim table belongs in the appendix or supplemental materials.

### Concern 5: The term "evidence-like" could invite confusion with audit evidence.

Risk:

Audit reviewers may worry that the paper blurs retrieved reported information and audit evidence.

Revision made:

Replaced "evidence-like materials" with "retrieved materials" in the introduction.

### Concern 6: The paper needs a stronger bridge between reporting checklist and the central validity framework.

Risk:

The reporting section could still be viewed as generic reproducibility guidance.

Revision made:

Added a compact table mapping reporting items to selection, representation, stability, traceability, and separability.

## Current Accept-Level Assessment

After revision, the v0 draft is stronger as a coherent methodology paper. The main contribution is now clearer:

> Retrieval-environment validity changes how researchers should design, evaluate, and report LLM-based audit studies because retrieval systems create the information environment from which LLM outputs are interpreted.

The draft is not yet ready for journal submission because it still needs:

1. verified citations;
2. expert review or carefully bounded treatment of audit-valid and integrated coding;
3. polished tables and figures;
4. appendix package;
5. final overclaiming review.

## Next Step

Proceed to either:

1. citation strengthening for the literature review, or
2. table and figure drafting for the main manuscript.

Given reviewer risk, the highest-value next step is citation strengthening. The draft's core argument is now coherent, but the literature positioning must demonstrate novelty relative to top accounting and auditing work.

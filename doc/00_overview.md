# Project Overview

## Working Title

**Retrieval as Research Design in LLM-Based Audit Research: Retrieval-Environment Validity and XBRL-Augmented Retrieval**

## Central Research Question

How does retrieval design affect the validity of inferences drawn from LLM-based audit research?

## Core Thesis

In retrieval-augmented LLM audit studies, retrieval design is part of the research design because retrieval systems dynamically select, rank, transform, and present the information environment observed by the model. This dynamic retrieval layer creates retrieval-specific threats to construct validity.

The paper introduces **retrieval-environment validity** as a diagnostic lens for evaluating whether the dynamically retrieved information environment aligns with the audit construct the researcher intends to study.

## Article Identity

This is a methodological paper on retrieval-created information environments in LLM-based audit research.

It is not:

- an XBRL ontology construction paper
- an LLM audit automation paper
- a GraphRAG performance paper
- a claim that XBRL is independent audit evidence
- a claim that hybrid retrieval is universally superior

## Primary Contribution

The primary contribution is the concept of **retrieval-environment validity**.

Supporting tools operationalize this concept:

| Tool | Function |
|---|---|
| Retrieval typology | Identifies retrieval design choices |
| Claim-level correctness protocol | Evaluates output validity |
| Failure mode taxonomy | Diagnoses retrieval-related threats |
| Reporting checklist | Supports transparency and reproducibility |
| Methodological demonstration | Illustrates inferential consequences |

## Final Novelty Statement

The novelty is not that XBRL can be retrieved by LLMs or that hybrid retrieval may improve output quality. The novelty is the methodological claim that retrieval-created information environments shape and constrain the inferences researchers can draw from LLM-based audit studies.

## Reviewer-Safe Positioning

The paper does not claim that XBRL retrieval improves audit judgment. It claims that without specifying and validating the retrieval-created information environment, researchers risk drawing invalid conclusions about LLM audit performance and audit judgment.

# Literature Positioning

## Core Differentiation Paragraph

Prior XBRL studies use structured reporting data to construct firm- or disclosure-level variables. Prior textual analysis studies convert accounting narratives into measures. Prior audit analytics studies examine how auditors process large and complex data. Prior LLM accounting studies evaluate model capabilities or AI use in reporting. This paper differs by moving the unit of analysis to the LLM study design itself and showing that retrieval systems dynamically construct the information environment from which audit-related inferences are drawn.

## AJPT Methodology Literature

AJPT has a tradition of methodological guidance papers that provide roadmaps, quality criteria, and design advice for audit research methods.

Representative streams include:

- qualitative auditing research roadmaps
- field research quality criteria
- audit judgment and decision-making design guidance

Contribution relative to this stream:

> This paper provides methodological guidance for a new class of audit studies: retrieval-augmented LLM audit studies.

## XBRL Literature

Prior XBRL research often uses XBRL as a structured reporting data source to construct firm-level or disclosure-level measures, such as:

- accounting reporting complexity
- tag counts
- extension tag use
- financial statement disaggregation
- disclosure structure
- reporting quality proxies

Contribution relative to this stream:

> Prior XBRL studies use structured reporting data to construct firm- or disclosure-level variables. This paper uses XBRL relations to construct and evaluate retrieval-created information environments in LLM-based audit research.

Key distinction:

> from the reporting firm to the LLM study design.

## Textual Analysis and LLM Accounting Literature

Textual analysis research asks how accounting narratives can be converted into measures. LLM accounting research often asks whether models can analyze, extract, classify, or predict accounting information.

Contribution relative to this stream:

> This paper asks what audit researchers can validly infer from LLM outputs given the retrieval-created information environment.

Key distinction:

> from text as data to retrieval as research design.

## Audit Analytics and Big Data Literature

Audit analytics and Big Data research often asks how auditors process large and complex datasets, whether such data can serve as complementary audit evidence, and how analytics affect audit efficiency, effectiveness, or judgment.

Contribution relative to this stream:

> In LLM-RAG audit studies, the methodological issue arises before judgment: the retrieval system selects, ranks, transforms, and presents the information environment that the model observes.

## AI and Audit Technology Literature

AI and audit technology research often studies:

- AI adoption by audit firms
- effects on audit quality and efficiency
- explainability and documentation
- audit workflow transformation
- model capability on accounting tasks

Contribution relative to this stream:

> This paper does not evaluate AI adoption or model capability. It evaluates the validity of the retrieval-created information environment used in LLM-based audit research.

## Reference Anchors

Use these literature anchors when drafting:

- AJPT methodology papers: qualitative, field, and audit JDM methodological guidance
- XBRL papers: XBRL review, accounting reporting complexity, disaggregation
- Textual analysis papers: accounting text as data, textual analysis validation
- Audit analytics papers: Big Data, audit evidence, analytics in financial statement audits
- AI/LLM accounting papers: LLM applications, generative AI in reporting, financial statement analysis with LLMs

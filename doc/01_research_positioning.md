# Research Positioning

## One-Sentence Positioning

This paper moves from **text as data** and **XBRL as data** to **retrieval as research design**.

## Why This Is an Audit Methodology Paper

Prior audit research has long recognized that information environments affect judgment and decision-making. Retrieval-augmented LLM audit studies introduce a distinct version of this problem because the information environment is not simply assigned by the researcher ex ante. Instead, it is dynamically constructed by retrieval systems at runtime.

The methodological issue is therefore not only what the LLM says, but what information environment the retriever created before the LLM produced that output.

## Unit of Analysis Shift

Prior related literatures often focus on:

- the reporting firm
- the disclosure
- the auditor
- the audit firm
- the model
- the benchmark task

This paper shifts the unit of analysis to:

> the LLM study design itself, specifically the retrieval-created information environment.

## Core Research Design Claim

In retrieval-augmented LLM audit studies, retrieval design is part of the research design.

This is because retrieval design shapes:

- what information the LLM sees
- whether construct-relevant information is retrieved
- whether retrieved information is faithfully represented
- whether output claims can be traced to retrieved sources
- whether model effects can be separated from retrieval effects

## Modest Claim

The objective is not to prescribe a universally preferred retrieval architecture, but to help audit researchers align retrieval design with research constructs and limit inferential overreach when interpreting LLM outputs.

## Safe Causal Language

Prefer:

- retrieval design shapes inference
- retrieval design constrains inference
- retrieval design bounds inference
- retrieval design affects what researchers can validly infer

Avoid:

- retrieval design determines audit truth
- XBRL retrieval proves audit validity
- hybrid retrieval is best
- graph-valid means audit-valid
